## 2.1 Competitive landscape awareness: **5 / 15**

**Justification:** The market research names plenty of alternatives. The brief's differentiation is specific, but the research contradicts it. The brief says it differs from location-first products, yet the research shows several products that already put one artist's whole tour on a map, Bandsintown among them. None of those are named in the brief. The one differentiator left standing is distance from any location the user enters, and the research found no evidence for or against it. So the differentiation is not tied to the alternatives that actually exist.

**Evidence the brief offers:**
> "Existing live-event discovery products (Bandsintown, Songkick) are built around **"what's on near me?"** ... This idea inverts it: **start with who you want to see, then work out visually where you can see them.**" (tour-map.md, Positioning)

> "Where an artist isn't playing anywhere local, the list-and-location products simply show nothing, while the map still shows the nearest options worth travelling for." (tour-map.md, Positioning)

**Evidence in the market research that undercuts it:**
> "**Bandsintown artist-page event map** - Artist pages gained an event map (powered by Stay22) showing all upcoming shows. Fans can view the whole tour or select dates to see shows in a range ... Changelog dated 2022"

> "**Tour Radius** - Product Hunt listing describes an interactive map where you search any artist to explore their full tour: dates, venues, sold-out status and every stop on the route."

> "**ROSTR Pro "Live" tab** - Uses Bandsintown data to generate an interactive map of an artist's upcoming shows." / "**MusicMap** - Enter a musician and it shows an interactive map of past and upcoming concerts." / "**Concert Map** ..." / "**Artist Map (open source)** ..." / "The Dave Matthews Band app (iTour.dmb) offers an interactive tour map."

**Where some differentiation may remain (not sourced):**
> "Bandsintown: the search found no evidence of a distance-from-arbitrary-location filter on artist pages ... Treat as no sourced evidence found for or against."

> "A Consequence concerts page lists a distance from the user's location per event." (search summary only)

**What the score rests on:** "Artist-first map" is the brief's main differentiator, and the alternatives named in the research already do it. What may still be distinct is distance from any location the user enters (the gift-buyer case) and building the map from a pasted URL. The brief never sets these against Tour Radius or the Bandsintown event map. The research could not confirm whether those products already offer them. The brief also ignores an alternative the research names for gift buyers: "Gift vouchers instead of choosing a date ... let the recipient choose show and date." That fits the 5 anchor: "differentiation isn't tied to a real alternative". It does not reach the 10 anchor, because the named alternatives are named in the research, not engaged in the brief.

## 2.2 Market timing / signal: **7 / 10**

**Justification:** The research has sourced market-size and trend data for UK live music, plus a sourced figure on how many people buy tickets as gifts. But all of it is about the market in general. None of it shows demand for the specific job: finding a reachable date on one artist's tour, especially for someone in another city. Demand for the product is inferred from a growing market, not sourced. The brief itself also says it has no timing case.

**Sourced signal present:**
> "LIVE reports UK live music consumer spend of an estimated £7.6bn in 2025, up 12.9% year on year."

> "Average UK fan spent £318 on live tickets over 12 months (18 to 24 year olds £425)."

> "YouGov (February 2023): 10% of respondents said they bought tickets as presents"

**Gaps that keep it below 10:**
> "No sourced evidence found on the share of gig tickets bought for someone in a different city."

> "Tour Radius ... Pricing, data source and whether it is still live were not confirmed"

There is adjacent product traction, but it is weak evidence: several of the alternatives look like hobby projects ("this is a github.io page, which suggests a hobby project"), and a fan only proposed a map in a Reddit thread.

**The brief's own position:**
> "This is a side project scratching that itch, not a bet on market timing." (tour-map.md, Why now)

**What the score rests on:** The market-level figures are sourced, so this is above the 3 anchor ("asserted with no supporting signal"). The signal for this job and this segment is missing or inferred, and the gift figure covers tickets of all kinds, not gig tickets bought for someone in another city. That puts it between 7 and 10, and the rubric says to take the lower anchor, so 7.

## Substance gate (Market / Competitive lens): **4 / 10**

Scored last. This is reported separately and is not part of the subtotal.

**Trace 1 (fails): "Bandsintown and Songkick are location-first, and they show nothing when the artist isn't playing locally."**
The brief makes this claim about the market, and its whole positioning section depends on it. The research says the opposite for Bandsintown: since 2022, Bandsintown artist pages have had an event map "showing all upcoming shows. Fans can view the whole tour". Bandsintown data also feeds Apple Music, Apple Maps and Shazam ("Apple Music, Apple Maps and Shazam concert discovery use Bandsintown data"). For Songkick, the research found "no sourced evidence found on single-artist map", so the claim is unsupported there. On the research's own evidence, the brief's characterisation of its main named competitor is wrong.

**Trace 2 (partly supported): "Gift buyers buying for someone elsewhere are the case where list views fail worst."**
The research supports a gifting segment existing ("10% of respondents said they bought tickets as presents"). It does not support the specific case: "No sourced evidence found on the share of gig tickets bought for someone in a different city." The research also names an alternative for this segment, vouchers that "let the recipient choose show and date", and the brief does not mention it. This part of the claim is stated, not supported.

**Strongest argument that the idea is wrong, from a market angle:**
The research says tour pages are "commonly published through embedded widgets", and Bandsintown's widget is one of the main ones. So the pages Tour Map would scrape mostly carry data that comes from Bandsintown. Bandsintown already offers an artist-first tour map built on that data, and its Partner Search API page says "not to scrape data or modify content". On that evidence, the product depends on its strongest competitor for supply, competes against a feature that competitor shipped in 2022, and its remaining differentiator (distance from any location the user enters) is something Bandsintown could add as a small change to its existing map. The brief does not anticipate this. It lists "Terms of use" as an open question, but it never names the Bandsintown event map, Tour Radius or the other map products. It also describes the scraping approach as "no dependency on a third-party events API or its licence", which the widget evidence undercuts.

**Score:** At least one sampled item contradicts the market research (Trace 1), and the strongest counter-argument is not addressed. That matches the 4 anchor.

**Note for synthesis:** In this lens, the Substance gate is at or below 4. Under the rubric's gate rule, that means this lens's sampled trace failed: the brief's competitive positioning is contradicted by `ideation/market-research.md`.
