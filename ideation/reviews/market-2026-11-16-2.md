## 2.1 Competitive landscape awareness: **10 / 15**

**Justification:** The brief engages a real alternative from the research (Bandsintown's artist-page map), not a strawman. But its differentiation comes down to "easier to navigate". The research neither confirms nor contradicts that. The brief also never mentions the several existing single-artist, map-first tools the research names. Those tools already offer the brief's core proposition ("the map is the page").

**Evidence:**

- The alternative is named, and the research confirms it exists. Brief: *"Bandsintown already offers an event map on its artist pages, but the map is secondary to the list, sits inside a wider discovery product, and is not the thing you land on."* Research: *"Changelog (March 2022): artist pages gained an event map powered by Stay22, showing all upcoming shows."* The research marks this "search summary only, unverified, dated 2022".
- The differentiation against Bandsintown is uncorroborated. Brief: *"From first-hand use, its map is **hard to navigate**."* Research: *"No sourced evidence found that singles out Bandsintown's artist-page map as hard to use."* and *"The product owner's first-hand view is therefore not corroborated or contradicted by any published source found."* The brief's structural claim ("secondary to the list ... not the thing you land on") is not sourced either. Research: *"Whether the map still exists in the same form in November 2026, how it frames the tour by default, and whether artists can turn it off: no sourced evidence found. The map was not viewed first-hand in this session."*
- The brief does not engage the alternatives that already offer the same proposition. Research:
  - Tour Radius: *"interactive map where you search any artist to see the full tour (dates, venues, sold-out status, route)"*
  - MusicMap: *"Enter a musician, see an interactive map of past and upcoming concerts"*
  - Concert Map, Artist Map (Songkick-fed), ROSTR Pro "Live" tab, Gigora, iTour.dmb
  - Ticketmaster-fed hobby maps: Gig Guru and the ureynrey *"concert-mapping app connected to Ticketmaster"*

  The brief names none of these. Its only differentiating line, *"rather than a list with a map tucked behind it"*, works against Bandsintown alone. Against Tour Radius and MusicMap, "map-first" is a feature they already have, not a difference. Every one of these products is marked unverified for liveness in the research, which lowers their weight but does not remove them.
- **Why not 15:** "Specific and credible against them" fails on credibility. Against Bandsintown, the difference is a first-hand impression with no supporting evidence. Against the map-first alternatives, there is no stated difference at all. **Why not 5:** Bandsintown is engaged properly, not "in passing". The brief also states the competitive position honestly: *"Bandsintown covers most of the job; this is unlikely to win users from it"*.
- Context, not scored up or down: the brief changes the goal from winning users to running an experiment (*"The product exists to test those interaction ideas, not to compete with Bandsintown for users."*). That makes weak differentiation less damaging to the idea. It does not change what this criterion measures.

## 2.2 Market timing / signal: **7 / 10**

**Justification:** The research has sourced market-size and gifting figures, but they cover the broad market and were carried over from v1 without re-checking. The research explicitly finds nothing for the specific demand this idea depends on: map versus list preference, and buying tickets for someone in another city. The brief itself disclaims any timing case.

**Evidence:**

- Broad market size (sourced, not re-verified). Research: *"UK live music consumer spend about £7.6bn in 2025, up 12.9%"* and *"YouGov (Feb 2023): 10% of respondents bought tickets as presents"*. The section heading reads *"carried from v1, not re-verified"*.
- Adjacent product evidence (unverified): Bandsintown chose to build an artist-page map in 2022, and several hobby and pro tour maps exist. The research adds: *"Evidence of any such app reaching real users or being reviewed for terms compliance: no sourced evidence found."*
- Gaps in the job-specific demand evidence. Research: *"Map vs list usability: no sourced evidence found. The search returned no study comparing the two."* and *"Share of gig tickets bought for someone in a different city: no sourced evidence found."*
- The brief's own position. Brief: *"Personal frustration ... This is a side project, not a market-timing bet."* That is honest, but it is not a signal.
- **Why not 10:** Linking a £7.6bn market to demand for a map-first tour view is an inference, not something the research sources. **Why not 3:** the research does contain sourced demand figures, and the brief doesn't claim timing it can't support.

## Substance gate (Market lens): **4 / 10**

**Sample 1: "Bandsintown's map is secondary to the list and hard to navigate" (brief, The problem). Does not hold up.**
The brief's differentiation rests on this claim. So does its success criterion (*"side by side with Bandsintown's map for the same tour, people find this one quicker and easier to navigate"*). In the research, every part of the claim comes back as no evidence:
- No published usability complaint was found.
- The default framing is unknown.
- Whether the map still exists in that form in November 2026 is unknown.
- The map was not viewed first-hand.

The brief does say where the claim comes from ("first-hand use"), and its validation path turns it into a test, which is to its credit. But the brief states it as fact in the problem statement, and the research does not support it. That is the 4-anchor case: "named in the document but not actually supported".

**Sample 2: "Pick an artist and you land on a map of the UK with a pin at every tour date", using Ticketmaster data (brief, Rough shape). Holds up, because the brief qualifies it.**
The research makes coverage a real constraint:
- *"Ticketmaster's own submission says it partners with five of twelve major UK arenas; the other seven sell through other providers."*
- Live Nation has 58% of UK primary sales (disputed).
- *"tour-date completeness versus artists' own listings: no sourced evidence found."*

So "a pin at every tour date" cannot be true for many tours. The brief anticipates this, though: *"only dates Ticketmaster sells or lists appear ... will show gaps"*. Its validation path includes *"Check early how many of those tours Ticketmaster covers in full"*. This one traces end to end.

**Strongest argument that this is wrong (market lens):**
The experiment measures the wrong comparison, and data will confound it.
1. "Map-first single-artist tour view" is not an untested interaction idea. The research names at least two existing tools built exactly that way: Tour Radius (*"search any artist to see the full tour"*) and MusicMap (*"Enter a musician, see an interactive map"*). It also names Ticketmaster-fed hobby maps. The brief's only comparison point is Bandsintown, so it never checks whether its framing, clustering and mobile ideas are already done, or done badly, elsewhere. Looking at those tools first is a cheaper learning step than building.
2. The planned side-by-side test pits a Ticketmaster-only map against Bandsintown, whose data is supplied by artists. Ticketmaster may cover about 5 of 12 major arenas. Testers may then prefer whichever map is complete, not whichever is easier to use, so the test could not separate a UX result from a data result.

The brief partly anticipates point 2: *"if most have large gaps, the data source, not the map, is the constraint"*. It does not anticipate point 1 anywhere.

**Why 4:** One of the two sampled items, the one carrying the differentiation and the success measure, is not supported by the research. The strongest counter-argument is only half-answered.

**What would lift it:**
- A dated first-hand record (screenshots or notes) of the Bandsintown artist map as it works in November 2026, so the "hard to navigate" baseline is evidence, not recollection.
- A short paragraph placing v2 against Tour Radius, MusicMap and Concert Map, even if only to say what this experiment tests that they don't.
- A side-by-side protocol that uses only tours with full Ticketmaster coverage, so data gaps don't confound the UX comparison.
