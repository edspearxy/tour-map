## 4.1 Named risks: 10 / 15

**Justification:** All three categories appear and each has a reason. But the legal/terms risk is built on clauses the research could not find, and leaves out the ones it did find. Map-tile licence risk is missing entirely. So the regulatory category does not hold up against the input well enough for the top anchor. Where the call was between two anchors, I scored down.

**Evidence by category:**

- **Technical: present, specific, and supported.**
  - Brief: *"Data coverage: Ticketmaster only shows its own and partner listings; some tours will be incomplete, which could make the map look wrong rather than just sparse."* The research supports it: *"Ticketmaster's own submission says it partners with five of twelve major UK arenas; the other seven sell through other providers."*
  - Brief: *"Artist matching: a name search can return the wrong act or tribute bands."* The research supports it: *"the `keyword` parameter matches an event's name, and partial words do not match."*
  - Brief: *"Venue location accuracy: relies on Ticketmaster's venue coordinates being present and correct."* The research supports it: *"Whether UK venues carry populated coordinates: no sourced evidence found."*
- **Business/adoption: present, with a reason.**
  - Brief: *"Market: Bandsintown covers most of the job; this is unlikely to win users from it, which is acceptable for a personal/learning project."* This is stated plainly and matches the stated purpose: *"The product exists to test those interaction ideas, not to compete with Bandsintown for users."*
- **Regulatory/legal: present, but it does not match the input.**
  - Brief: *"API dependency and terms: free tier is rate-limited (5,000 calls/day, 5/second) and governed by Ticketmaster's terms (attribution, caching, display rules) — these must be read before build; the key can be revoked."*
  - Against the research:
    - The three clauses the brief names are the three the research could not source: *"Attribution/branding, caching/storage limits, display restrictions, commercial vs non-commercial use: no sourced evidence found."*
    - The clauses the research *did* surface are not named:
      - *"the terms bar applications that replicate or try to replace the essential user experience of Ticketmaster.com"*
      - *"content Ticketmaster asks to be removed must come out of the application within 24 hours"*
      - *"Ticketmaster may change the terms without notice"*
    - The 24-hour removal duty matters for a side project with no one on call. The brief does name revocation (*"the key can be revoked"*), which the research supports.
  - Two other legal risks are missing:
    - The brief says *"affiliate ticket links possibly later"*. But commercial-use status under Ticketmaster's terms is unknown per the research, and the research notes *"MapTiler's own terms say the Free Plan is limited to non-commercial use"*. Adding affiliate links could therefore move the site out of free-tier terms on two providers. The brief does not say so.
    - It names no map tile provider and no tile-licence risk at all. The research records attribution duties and the OSM tile server's *"Blocking without notice is possible"*. For a product where *"the map is the page"*, that is a single point of failure as real as the API key.
  - The *"Not a copy"* paragraph does deal with the IP risk against Bandsintown (*"uses none of Bandsintown's name, branding, design or data"*). That counts in the brief's favour.

## 4.2 Reversibility / cheap validation path: 10 / 10

**Justification:** It gives a specific, small validation plan with an explicit stop rule. It also includes a cheap data check that can run before the UX work.

**Quoted source text:** *"Pick 3–5 real UK tours, use the map for real ticket decisions (my own and gifts), and show it to a few friends who go to gigs, asking them to find a given date on this map and on Bandsintown's. If the map-first view isn't clearly quicker than both the list and Bandsintown's map, stop before investing further. Check early how many of those tours Ticketmaster covers in full — if most have large gaps, the data source, not the map, is the constraint."*

**Caveats (not enough to change the anchor, but the author should fix them):**

- The coverage check says *"early"* without saying when. It should come explicitly before any map UI work, because it costs a handful of API calls against the artists' own published lists.
- The check has no stated consequence. *"the data source … is the constraint"* is a diagnosis, not a decision: it does not say whether to stop, switch source, or hand-enter data for the test tours.
- *"clearly quicker"* is not defined, so the stop rule can be argued either way after the fact.
- The Bandsintown comparison assumes its map still exists. The research says *"Whether the map still exists in the same form in November 2026 … no sourced evidence found."* The owner's first-hand use partly covers this. Confirming the map is still there takes one visit and should be step zero.

## Lens subtotal: 20 / 25

## Substance gate (Risk lens): 4 / 10

**Sampled item 1. The brief says Ticketmaster's coverage gaps are *"acceptable for an experiment, and visible to the user."* Does not hold up.**

- **The brief contradicts itself.** Its own risk section says incomplete tours *"could make the map look wrong rather than just sparse."*
- **The API cannot reveal its own gaps.** It returns only what Ticketmaster lists. Nothing in the brief shows how a user would know a date is missing: no comparison against the artist's list, no "partial tour" notice.
- **The research makes gaps likely.** It suggests gaps will be common, not rare: *"partners with five of twelve major UK arenas"*, and *"Share of Discovery API music events in UK, or tour-date completeness versus artists' own listings: no sourced evidence found."*
- **Even the brief's own example tour is unchecked.** That is Cardiff Utilita Arena, Eventim Apollo, Bristol Hippodrome and Halifax Piece Hall. Neither the brief nor the research checks whether any of them is served by Ticketmaster.
- **Conclusion.** The reason the coverage risk is called *acceptable* rests on a mitigation that the brief never supports and that its own risk text contradicts.

**Sampled item 2. The brief says the API is *"governed by Ticketmaster's terms (attribution, caching, display rules)"* with *"5,000 calls/day, 5/second"*. Holds only partly.**

- **The limits.** They match the research's figures, but the research marks those as unverified v1 defaults.
- **The clauses.** As described under 4.1, the brief names the clauses the research could not find and leaves out the ones it did find.
- **Credit where due.** The brief's *"these must be read before build"* is the right response, given that the research could not reach developer.ticketmaster.com. So this item is named and its mitigation is right. Its content is assumed, not traced.

**Strongest counter-argument (falsification):**

- **The argument.** The validation test cannot isolate the thing it claims to test. The idea is a *map-UX experiment*, but the comparison is against Bandsintown, whose listings are not limited to one ticket agent. Ticketmaster's are, and per the research it covers well under half of major UK arenas.
- **What goes wrong.** On a typical arena tour, testers asked to *"find a given date"* will either:
  - fail on dates the Ticketmaster map does not have, so data loses the test rather than the UX; or
  - be given only dates that are present, which biases the test towards the new map.
- **Result.** Either way, the result says nothing reliable about the map UX, which is the project's stated purpose.
- **Does the brief anticipate this?** Partly: *"if most have large gaps, the data source, not the map, is the constraint."* But it does not answer it. It has no fallback for keeping the UX test clean. Examples would be hand-checked test tours, or pre-selecting tours that Ticketmaster covers in full, compared against the artist's own list.

**Gate finding:** At least one sampled item (the "visible to the user" mitigation) is named but not supported, and the brief's own risk text contradicts it. That is the 4 anchor.

> Substance gate (Risk lens): sampled trace failed. The claim that coverage gaps are "visible to the user", which is the stated basis for accepting the coverage risk, has no mechanism behind it and is contradicted by the brief's own Risks section ("could make the map look wrong rather than just sparse"), against research showing Ticketmaster serves 5 of 12 major UK arenas. Under the rubric's gate rule, a score of 4 or below caps the verdict; synthesis applies that across lenses.

**What would lift it:**

1. Remove "visible to the user", or say how gaps are detected and shown, for example by checking against the artist's own list.
2. State that the coverage check runs before any UI work and what happens if it fails.
3. Make sure the side-by-side test uses tours confirmed as fully covered, so the result measures the map and not the data.
4. Name the clauses the research actually found (no replicating Ticketmaster's core experience, the 24-hour removal duty, terms changing without notice), the map tile provider's licence and blocking risk, and what affiliate links would do to commercial-use status.
