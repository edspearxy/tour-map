## 4. Disagreements and tensions between lenses

These are the points where one lens's findings undercut another lens's score. Each lens worked blind to the others, so these have not been reconciled anywhere else.

### D1. Risk 4.2 (10/10, full marks) is undercut by all four lenses' falsification, including Risk's own
- Risk gave the validation path full marks.
- Every lens's strongest counter-argument says that same validation test cannot produce a valid answer:
  - **Feasibility:** "the map-UX comparison it exists to make is confounded by data completeness... the 'given date' a tester is asked to find may simply be missing."
  - **Market:** "Testers may then prefer whichever map is complete, not whichever is easier to use."
  - **User-Value:** "a speed test measures less clutter as much as better UX."
  - **Risk's own Substance section:** "the result says nothing reliable about the map UX, which is the project's stated purpose."
- 4.2 scores whether the path is cheap and reversible, and it is. But a cheap test that can't isolate its variable is weaker than a 10 suggests.
- Risk listed the caveats (no timing, no consequence on failure, undefined "clearly quicker") and kept the top anchor anyway.
- This is the largest single tension in the readout. Taken at face value, the 10 is what lifts Risk to 20/25 and the total into the "Pursue with caveats" range. The human should weigh it.

### D2. Market treats map-first as already done. User-Value and Risk accept it as the thing being tested.
- **Market (falsification, point 1):** "'Map-first single-artist tour view' is not an untested interaction idea". Tour Radius and MusicMap already offer it, and looking at them first is "a cheaper learning step than building".
- **User-Value 3.2:** notes the same tools but "only noted this and not marked down further for it".
- **Risk:** does not mention them. Its 10/10 on 4.2 treats the build-and-compare plan as the cheapest validation available.
- The three lenses agree on the evidence: those tools are unverified as live. They give it different weight. Market's argument, if accepted, means a cheaper validation step exists that the brief's plan skips.

### D3. Feasibility found a technical unknown that Risk 4.1 does not name: UK API access
- **Feasibility 1.1:** "UK access isn't confirmed... No result confirmed GB specifically for attractions... the 'International Discovery API' is reported as no longer accepting new API key requests."
- **Risk 4.1** scores the technical category as "present, specific, and supported". It lists coverage, matching and coordinates, but not whether UK events are reachable at all on a standard key.
- This is the pattern the standards say to flag. Risk's technical credit doesn't account for an unknown that Feasibility raised and the brief doesn't mention.

### D4. Risk names a terms risk that Feasibility doesn't weigh: "replicating Ticketmaster's core experience"
- **Risk 4.1:** the research surfaced a clause barring applications that "replicate or try to replace the essential user experience of Ticketmaster.com", plus a 24-hour duty to remove content and terms that can change without notice.
- **Feasibility 1.1** treats the API as available "within its published terms" and does not consider whether a single-purpose event-finder raises the replication clause.
- Neither lens resolves this. It sits outside both scores.

### D5. "Coverage risk is named" (Risk 4.1 credit) versus "coverage makes the product worse than the status quo" (User-Value 3.2)
- **Risk 4.1** credits the coverage risk as named and backed by research.
- **User-Value 3.2** goes further: against the obvious alternative (the artist's own complete list), an incomplete map "gets the wrong answer faster". For a "which date is reachable" job, a missing date reads as "no show there".
- **Feasibility** treats coverage as a buildability limit.
- The three lenses describe the same fact at different severities. Only User-Value scores its effect on the job itself.

### D6. Feasibility's Substance score (7) versus the other three (4)
- This is a difference in what was sampled, not a disagreement over the same item.
- Where samples overlapped ("a pin at every tour date"), Feasibility and Market reached the same conclusion: it holds because the brief qualifies it.
- On falsification, all four lenses independently reached the same counter-argument (the side-by-side test is confounded by Ticketmaster's partial coverage). That convergence is a stronger signal than any single score.

### Where the lenses agree (no tension)
- The map side can be built.
- The brief is honest about not competing with Bandsintown.
- The Bandsintown "hard to navigate" claim is first-hand only. Feasibility, Market, User-Value and Risk all note this.
- Ticketmaster's UK coverage (about 5 of 12 major arenas) is the central constraint.
- Missing legal items: the map tile provider and licence, and affiliate links changing commercial-use status under both Ticketmaster and MapTiler. Feasibility notes these as effort gaps and Risk as missing legal risks. They describe them consistently.

## 6. What would lift the Substance gate

All three lenses at 4 need to rise above 4. Their recommendations overlap, so they are grouped below by which lens each one lifts.

**Fixes that lift several lenses at once:**
1. **Remove "visible to the user", or make it true** (User-Value, Risk). For example, show a "Ticketmaster-listed dates only; check the artist's site for the full tour" notice, or check the dates against the artist's own list. Then say what the user sees when their nearest date is missing.
2. **Control the side-by-side test for data completeness** (all four lenses). Use only tours confirmed as fully covered by Ticketmaster against the artist's own list, or count and match the pins on each map. Define "clearly quicker".
3. **Run the coverage check first and attach a consequence to it** (Risk, Feasibility). Do it before any map UI work. If it fails, decide in advance whether to stop, switch source, or hand-enter the test tours. The brief's example tour is a natural first case: Cardiff Utilita Arena, Eventim Apollo, Bristol Hippodrome and Halifax Piece Hall.

**Market lens:**
4. Make a dated first-hand record (screenshots or notes) of Bandsintown's artist-page map as it works in November 2026. This turns "hard to navigate" from recollection into a baseline, and confirms the map still exists (Risk calls this "step zero").
5. Add a short paragraph placing v2 against Tour Radius, MusicMap and Concert Map, saying what this experiment tests that they don't.

**User-Value lens:**
6. Either back the gift-buyer "lives elsewhere" segment with evidence or drop it.

**Risk lens (affects 4.1 as well as Substance):**
7. Name the terms clauses the research actually found: no replicating Ticketmaster's core experience, the 24-hour removal duty, and terms changing without notice.
8. Name a map tile provider and its licence and blocking risk.
9. State what affiliate links would do to commercial-use status.

**Not raised as a Substance item, but unresolved (Feasibility):**
10. Confirm that a standard Discovery v2 key returns UK (GB) events. Decide whether API calls run in the browser or through a server-side cache, given the rate limits.
