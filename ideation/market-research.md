## Market Signal

### Map UX patterns and research (all from search summaries, not read in full)
- **Clustering when few points:** a vendor help page says clustering suits many locations in one region and is generally unnecessary for a few dispersed points (https://support.metalocator.com/en/articles/1659899-map-marker-clustering). Studio Wolf says not to cluster when the exact point matters to the user, and notes clusters sit at the centroid of their points (https://studiowolf.com/blog/to-cluster-or-not-to-cluster). A developer essay argues clustering hides data and adds clicks, zooms and pans (https://dev.to/markosmilja/beyond-marker-clustering-1jmj). A PeerJ preprint says practitioners over-use clustering to fix performance without resolving perceived performance (https://peerj.com/preprints/27858). Write-ups describe turning clustering off at a set zoom level, and expanding a cluster on hover (https://medium.com/homes-engineering/map-marker-clusters-c90b2f192324, search summary only). No Nielsen Norman Group material on clustering was found.
- **Default framing / fit-bounds:** Leaflet's reference documents `fitBounds(bounds, options?)` (https://leafletjs.com/reference.html). No MapLibre `fitBounds` source was found in this run. No sourced usability research on default framing for small point sets was found.
- **Mobile map interaction:** Google's blog says an embedded map captures swipes and can trap mobile users. Its fix is cooperative gesture handling (one finger scrolls the page, two fingers pan the map). It also made the fullscreen control visible by default on mobile because user studies found small embedded maps hard to use (https://maps-apis.googleblog.com/2016/11/smart-scrolling-comes-to-mobile-web-maps.html, dated 2016). Cooperative gesture options are documented for Google (https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/javascript/examples/interaction-cooperative) and Mapbox GL JS (https://docs.mapbox.com/mapbox-gl-js/example/cooperative-gestures.md).
- **Map vs list usability:** no sourced evidence found. The search returned no study comparing the two.

### Map provider costs and terms for a small hobby site (secondary sources unless stated; check live pages)
- **Mapbox GL JS:** sources agree on 50,000 free map loads per month; a load is counted when a Map object initialises, and pan/zoom do not add loads. Overage is reported at about $5 per 1,000, though tiers differ between sources. Whether a credit card is needed is disputed between sources. Official pricing page seen only as a partial snippet. Sources: https://www.mapbox.com/pricing/, https://docs.mapbox.com/accounts/guides/pricing/, https://www.woosmap.com/blog/mapbox-pricing (2026), https://storerocket.io/learn/mapbox-pricing. Mapbox GL JS moved to a non-open-source licence in December 2020 (https://www.geoapify.com/mapbox-gl-new-license-and-6-free-alternatives).
- **MapTiler Cloud:** third-party listings give the free plan as 100,000 API requests and 5,000 API sessions per month; storage figures conflict. MapTiler's own terms say the Free Plan is limited to non-commercial use plus R&D, with on-screen attribution required. Per its docs, going over on the free plan suspends maps for the rest of the month rather than charging. Sources: https://www.maptiler.com/cloud/terms/, https://frontdeskreview.com/software/maps-api/maptiler/ (updated June 2026), https://freetier.co/directory/products/maptiler. Official numbers not read.
- **OpenStreetMap standard tile server (tile.openstreetmap.org):** the policy (per search summary and secondary sources) requires a valid identifying User-Agent (library defaults not recommended) and a Referer for websites. It requires visible attribution, and heavy use needs permission. Blocking without notice is possible, and the policy recommends running your own tile server. Attribution wording and caching details were uncertain. Sources: https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/, https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Blocked_tiles, https://docs.fleaflet.dev/tile-servers/using-openstreetmap-direct
- **Google Maps Platform:** since 1 March 2025 the $200 monthly credit was replaced by a free monthly threshold per Core Services SKU: 10,000 billable events for Essentials SKUs, 5,000 Pro, 1,000 Enterprise. What counts as an event varies by SKU. Billing account required (not stated in the summary; verify). Sources: https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/march-2025, https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/faq
- **Leaflet and MapLibre GL JS:** MapLibre GL JS is a free, open-source fork of mapbox-gl-js. Package metadata lists BSD-3-Clause. Sources: https://www.npmjs.com/package/maplibre-gl, https://classic.yarnpkg.com/en/package/maplibre-gl. Leaflet is described as open source (docs: https://leafletjs.com/reference.html). Leaflet's licence text was not retrieved. Both libraries need a tile or style source from a provider above.

## Ticketmaster Discovery API as MVP data source

developer.ticketmaster.com did not resolve in this session, so everything here is from search summaries (labelled unverified) and the full terms text was not read.

### Terms of use relevant to a small free hobby site
- **Terms document and date:** "General Terms of Use", last updated 27 June 2023 per the search summary. Source: https://developer.ticketmaster.com/support/terms-of-use/ (unverified, dated 2023, snippet only).
- **Licence scope:** API licensed on a worldwide (except as limited), non-exclusive, non-sublicenseable, non-transferable basis; unstated rights reserved by Ticketmaster. Same source (unverified, dated 2023).
- **Compliance with consumer terms:** use is conditioned on complying with the Ticketmaster Terms of Use at ticketmaster.com; ticket purchases made through the API are governed by those terms. Same source (unverified, dated 2023).
- **Restricted uses:** the terms bar applications that replicate or try to replace the essential user experience of Ticketmaster.com or its apps, and use that adversely affects Ticketmaster server stability. Same source (unverified, dated 2023).
- **Removal duty:** content Ticketmaster asks to be removed must come out of the application within 24 hours. Same source (unverified, dated 2023).
- **Key revocation / termination:** rights end automatically on breach, on written notice from Ticketmaster, or if Ticketmaster disables API access; Ticketmaster may change the terms without notice. Same source (unverified, dated 2023).
- **Rate limiting of non-user-driven calls:** a third-party analysis says the terms allow limiting or blocking applications making large volumes of calls not primarily in response to direct user actions. Secondary, unverified. Default limits in v1: 5,000 calls/day and 5 requests/second, higher limits case by case after checks on terms, branding and representation of data (https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/getting-started/, unverified).
- **Attribution/branding, caching/storage limits, display restrictions, commercial vs non-commercial use:** no sourced evidence found. The searches did not return those clauses. A third-party guide only advises reusing results rather than querying per visitor, which is not an official rule (https://freeapihub.com/apis/ticketmaster-discovery). The Partner API is a separate programme restricted to companies with existing official distribution relationships (https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/partner/, unverified).
- **International Discovery API:** a separate product, reported as no longer accepting new API key requests (https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/international-discovery/v2, unverified, search summary).

### How artists are searched, and venue coordinates
- **Events endpoint:** the `keyword` parameter matches an event's name, and partial words do not match (docs example: "Mado" will not find "Madonna"). `attractionId` takes one or more comma-separated attraction IDs. Source: https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/discovery-api/v2 (unverified, search summary).
- **Attractions endpoint:** `/discovery/v2/attractions` finds attractions (artists, sports teams, packages, plays) filterable by name. A third-party gateway doc shows `attractions.json?keyword=Taylor+Swift` and pagination. Sources: https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/discovery-api/v2, https://lava.so/docs/gateway/providers/ticketmaster (unverified).
- **Country filter:** `countryCode` is listed as a filter; Microsoft's connector documents it for attractions. No result confirmed GB specifically for attractions. Sources: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/connectors/ticketmaster (unverified), https://apify.com/axlymxp/ticketmaster-events-scraper (third-party scraper; lists GB as an example code).
- **Music filter:** official events examples use `classificationName=music`. No source confirmed how it combines with keyword on attractions.
- **Paging cap:** size multiplied by page must be below 1000 per a secondary summary (https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/getting-started/, unverified).
- **Venue coordinates:** venue objects include a `location` with `latitude` and `longitude`; the v1 docs describe them as strings and a third-party sample shows numbers. The official v2 page lists parameters but not the full response schema, so the field types come from third-party sites. `latlong` is deprecated in favour of `geoPoint` (geoHash). Sources: https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/discovery-manual/v2, https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/apis/discovery-api/v2, https://lava.so/docs/gateway/providers/ticketmaster (all unverified, summary only). Whether UK venues carry populated coordinates: no sourced evidence found.
- **Official tutorial:** "Locate Events on a Map" uses the Discovery API with Google Maps and event markers (https://developer.ticketmaster.com/products-and-docs/tutorials/events-search/search_events_in_location.html, unverified, summary only).

### UK ticketing market share and coverage
- **2008 baseline:** Competition Commission inquiry put Ticketmaster's UK share at 40-50%, with See Tickets (next largest) about half that size. Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5543808240f0b6158900004a/Ticketmaster_-_Seatwave_full_text_decision.pdf and related CMA documents (unverified, dated 2008 per the search summary; documents not opened).
- **2025 parliamentary figures:** a May 2026 committee report (HC 128) attributes 58% of UK primary ticket sales to Live Nation (66% including affiliates), out of 23.1 million UK tickets sold in 2025. Live Nation disputes the estimates. Sources: https://www.beyondthevote.co.uk/committee-publication/b3b8e2c9-4bfa-4ac4-9858-69a820cee37f (unverified, dated 2026), https://news.pollstar.com/2026/04/24/business-as-usual-repercussions-of-live-nation-trial-in-europe-the-uk/. Ticketmaster's UK chief told a 2025 committee he had no current share estimate (summary of parliamentary evidence, https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/161921/pdf/, unverified, dated 2025).
- **Competitors named:** Live Nation says Ticketmaster faces AXS, CTS Eventim, See Tickets, DICE and others; DICE is said to have a small share but the highest user satisfaction. Same committee sources (unverified).
- **Arena coverage:** Ticketmaster's own submission says it partners with five of twelve major UK arenas; the other seven sell through other providers. Source: https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/161921/pdf/ (unverified, dated 2025, summary only; which arenas not stated in the summary).
- **Regulatory status:** CMA took enforcement action over the 2024 Oasis sale; MPs have urged a full market investigation, and as of October 2026 the CMA had given no new timeline. Sources: https://www.ticketnews.com/2026/10/uk-cma-live-nation-investigation-timeline/ (unverified, dated 2026), https://www.musicweek.com/public_html/index.php/live/read/mps-urge-cma-to-investigate-live-nation-s-market-power/095279.
- **Gigantic:** no sourced UK share data found. Share figures for See Tickets, Dice, AXS individually: no sourced evidence found.
